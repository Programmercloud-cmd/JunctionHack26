Heres a previous analysis of previous junction hackathons: Junction’s recent winners ranged from a four-person team simulating radio waves to a solo builder turning equipment reporting into a game. The pattern I’d take into this weekend is to **pick a specific problem your team can make visible in a working demo**. That matters at Junction because other hackers choose the five overall finalists before the grand jury sees them. [GitHub](https://github.com/ron0studios/WAVELET?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

## What you’ll face this weekend

**Junction runs 13–15 November at Hype Areena in Espoo**, with teams of **one to four**. Friday doors open at **16:00**, the opening ceremony is at **18:00**, and hacking starts at **19:30**. Final submissions are due **Sunday at 10:00**, giving you **38½ hours** from kickoff. There’s also a Friday-night preliminary submission. The platform says **23:55** and the event site says **23:59**, so I’d work to **23:55**. All times are local. [2026.hackjunction.com](https://2026.hackjunction.com/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Partners choose their own challenge winners using their own criteria. Face-to-face peer review selects the **top five overall**, who pitch to the grand jury on Sunday. The published tracks cover hardware, healthcare, AI infrastructure, fintech, agentic AI and data. Some tech challenges can be entered alongside your main track. [2026.hackjunction.com](https://2026.hackjunction.com/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

I’d use Friday’s sponsor conversations to find out **what they want to see working on Sunday** and which data, hardware or APIs you can actually access that night. Then aim to have **one end-to-end demo by Saturday evening**. For peer review, rehearse a version that starts with the user’s problem and gets to the working prototype in under a minute. If you want a concrete benchmark before Friday, WAVELET’s repository has screenshots and its submission presentation.
